Captain Care, an AI assistant for people who don't yet know what to ask

Live on careinsurance.com. Retrieval-augmented assistant built on Claude Haiku.

Problem
Insurance vocabulary stops people before they start. Visitors moved between plan pages without knowing which questions mattered, and the only alternative was a phone call.
What I did
Grounded the model on a curated corpus of brochures, terms and conditions, exclusions and brand guidelines, retrieved at query time. Built the intent taxonomy from 3,000+ call-recording transcripts and live-chat logs, so it matches how customers actually phrase things rather than how the catalogue is organised. Wrote the guardrails with Compliance and InfoSec: IRDAI circulars encoded as refusal conditions, scope held to health insurance, no invented products. The interface opens by asking for a name, keeps a standing AI disclosure, and carries thumbs up and down on every answer so the corpus improves from real usage.

2,548 customer conversations and 153 qualified leads since launch, a 6% visit-to-lead ratio, with zero compliance incidents.

A recommendation engine that picks the plan so the customer doesn't have to

Premium calculator on careinsurance.com, feeding a recommendation page.

Problem
Funnel and session analysis showed drop-off wasn't page friction, it was uncertainty. People were being asked to choose between plans they couldn't tell apart.
What I did
Redesigned the journey around decision support. The engine takes location and number of members, retrieves and ranks quotes across products at runtime, pre-selects the most suitable plan and states why, and holds a live premium summary next to a three-way comparison. Sum insured and tenure stay editable, and the ranking updates on every change.

Straight-through processing improved 45% for Travel Insurance and 15% for Health Insurance.

A quote page built for the decision, not the click

New quote-details page for Ultimate Care.

Problem
The old quote page listed options. It didn't tell a first-time buyer why 25 lakh matters more in Gurgaon than in a smaller town, what a quarterly plan costs across a year, or which add-ons are worth taking.
What I did
Rebuilt the page so every choice carries its reasoning. Sum insured is recommended by city tier, with a panel showing what local hospital bills actually look like. Payment type compares total outlay for quarterly against one-time. Add-ons are priced and explained inline. Features are grouped by what they cover, with the brochure one tap away.

Every element answers "why this?" before the customer has to ask. Shipped with UAT governance and post-release monitoring.

Product journeys driven by a rule engine, not by code

Care Supreme on product.careinsurance.com, one of the products configured on the engine.

Problem
Premium sourcing logic was hardcoded. Launching or changing a product meant an engineering cycle measured in weeks, and every product journey was a one-off build.
What I did
Replaced the hardcoded logic with an API-driven premium engine and a rule layer above it. Eligibility, sum insured bands, tenure options, tenure discounts and add-on pricing all come from configuration and resolve at runtime, so the same engine powers the recommendation page, the quote page and each product journey. On Care Supreme every option a customer can touch is a rule: the sum insured bands from 10 lakh to 1 crore, the 7.5% and 10% multi-year discounts, and the premium and discount lines that recalculate in the summary on each change.

Per-product onboarding cut from weeks to days.
